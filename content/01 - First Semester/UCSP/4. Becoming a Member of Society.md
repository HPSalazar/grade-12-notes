# Becoming a Member of Society

---

## Socialization

Socialization is a continuing process of social interaction through which people acquire their identities and skills in society.  
- It establishes our identity.  
- Guides us on how to act in socially acceptable ways.  
- Allows us to acquire culture that improves us as individuals.  

---

## Sociologist Views on Socialization

### Sigmund Freud – Psychoanalytic Theory
> [!quote] Freud’s View
> The unconscious mind shapes behavior. The formation of the self occurs between the **id** and the **superego**. Society’s influence is mainly seen in the superego.  

- **Conscious mind** – things we are aware of or can easily recall.  
- **Unconscious mind** – hidden wishes, desires, urges, and memories influencing behavior.  

### Freudian Theory
- **Id** – primitive part, source of urges (libidinal energy), fully unconscious.  
- **Ego** – deals with reality, ensures id’s demands are satisfied in safe and acceptable ways.  
- **Superego** – internalized morals and standards from family, parents, and society.

## Psychosexual Stages

> [!example] Freud’s Stages of Development
> Each stage focuses on different libidinal energies, conflicts, and manifestations.
> 1. Oral Stage
> 2. Anal Stage
> 3. Phallic Stage
> 4. Latency Stage
> 5. Genital Stage

1. **Oral Stage (0–1 y.o.)**  
   - Focus: Mouth (sucking, eating).  
   - Conflict: Weaning.  
   - Manifestations: Thumb sucking, overeating, dependency, excessive talking.  

2. **Anal Stage (1–3 y.o.)**  
   - Focus: Bowel control (toilet training).  
   - Conflict: Strict vs. lenient training.  
   - Manifestations:  
     - Strict training → perfectionist, controlling, stingy.  
     - Lenient training → messy, rebellious.  

3. **Phallic Stage (3–6 y.o.)**  
   - Focus: Genital awareness (gender identity).  
   - Conflict: Oedipus complex (boys), Electra complex (girls).  
   - Manifestations: Arrogance, difficulty with opposite sex, inferiority complex.  

4. **Latency Stage (6–12 y.o.)**  
   - Focus: Social development, little sexual drive.  
   - Manifestations: Shyness, poor social skills, lack of hobbies.  

5. **Genital Stage (12+ y.o.)**  
   - Focus: Mature intimacy and relationships.  
   - If unresolved earlier conflicts persist: difficulty with intimacy, fear of commitment, emotional immaturity.

## Other Theories of Social Development

### Jean Piaget – Cognitive Development Theory
> [!note] Children actively construct knowledge in 4 stages

1. **Sensorimotor (0–2)** – Learn via senses & movement. *Ex: Object permanence.*  
2. **Preoperational (2–7)** – Symbolic thinking, egocentrism. *Ex: Stick as sword, assuming others see the same.*  
3. **Concrete Operational (7–11)** – Logical thinking, conservation. *Ex: Tall vs. wide glass of water.*  
4. **Formal Operational (12+)** – Abstract, hypothetical reasoning. *Ex: Solutions to climate change.*  

---

### Lawrence Kohlberg – Moral Development Theory
> [!important] Moral reasoning develops through **3 levels, 6 stages**

**Level 1: Pre-conventional**  
- Stage 1: Obedience & Punishment (avoid punishment)  
- Stage 2: Individualism & Exchange (self-interest)  

**Level 2: Conventional**  
- Stage 3: Good Relationships (approval-seeking)  
- Stage 4: Maintaining Social Order (obey rules/laws)  

**Level 3: Post-conventional**  
- Stage 5: Social Contract (laws can be changed for justice)  
- Stage 6: Universal Principles (personal ethics > laws)  

---

### George Herbert Mead – Theory of the Social Self
> [!quote] The self develops through social interaction and role-taking.

- **Preparatory Stage** – Mimicking. *Ex: toddler uses phone like parent.*  
- **Play Stage** – Taking specific roles. *Ex: pretend doctor.*  
- **Game Stage** – Understanding multiple roles/rules. *Ex: basketball team play.*  

---

### Charles Cooley – Looking-Glass Self
> [!tip] Our self-image is shaped by how we think others see and judge us.

1. **Imagine how we appear to others** – *Ex: student presenting in class.*  
2. **Imagine others’ judgment** – *Ex: classmates’ reactions.*  
3. **Develop self-feeling** – *Ex: pride or embarrassment.*

## Agents of Socialization

### 1. Family
- Most important agent.  
- Parents/guardians teach survival skills, values, and norms.  

### 2. Schools
- Teach subject knowledge and life skills (discipline, deadlines).  
- Provide social interaction with teachers, staff, and peers.  

### 3. Peer Groups
- Allow independence from adults.  
- Peak influence during adolescence.  
- Affect short-term interests (unlike family’s long-term influence).  

### 4. Mass Media & Technology
> [!note] Mass Media’s Role
> - Influences attitudes and behavior.  
> - Connects people (“media” = middle).  
> - Bias can occur in event/story selection and coverage.  

### 5. Workplace
- Socialization through onboarding (meetings, orientations, training).  
- Personality traits (proactive, curious) influence adjustment speed.  
- Employees learn norms, procedures, and culture from peers.  

### 6. Religion
- Provides cultural systems, beliefs, spirituality, and moral values.  
- Sociology of religion studies beliefs, practices, and organizational forms.
